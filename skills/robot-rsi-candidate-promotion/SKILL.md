# RSI Candidate Promotion and Frozen Acceptance

Take a parent and candidate revision, a record of what changed, per-pair outcomes, retained-task requirements and the history of where feedback was used. Deliver a decision to inherit, retain the parent, or collect missing evidence, plus the scope of that decision.

1. **Allocate feedback by purpose.** Iterate on permitted development tasks. Choose the candidate on separate validation groups. Reserve a sealed acceptance set for the frozen candidate. Track exposure through videos, scores, logs, prompts and trajectory labels for every ancestor and component. Once an acceptance result has informed a change or selected a version, mark the affected evidence as development or selection data. Check whether the protocol permits any reuse of exposed feedback. If it prohibits such development, isolate the logs and the affected candidate from the submission lineage. Return to a clean ancestor and permitted external feedback. Swapping seeds or using a fresh split cannot cure the prohibited tuning.
2. **Predeclare promotion criteria.** Define the independent unit, primary full-task outcome, minimum gain, allowed retention losses, intervention budget and stopping rule. Compare the candidate with both its immediate parent and, periodically, a fixed anchor across generations. Report gains, losses, missing pairs and per-task effects on previously solved and newly targeted tasks. Reusing a validation panel across many rounds makes the selected gain optimistic. Reserve fresh confirmation groups or bound the comparison budget. [Stage-specific updates and retention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06654v1)
   Select candidates on layouts and seeds disjoint from final reporting. Keep internal sealed layouts inaccessible to the agent. Have a separate evaluator run the frozen systems and log every access to sealed layouts. Pin the evaluation framework to an exact commit each round and record it with both system manifests. [Hidden verification precedent](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434#S3.SS3) · [Git revision verification](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-rev-parse) · Source pending for the exact internal access and logging protocol.
   To claim a benefit from repeated feedback, compare the sequence of inherited revisions with a one-shot or fixed-collection baseline at matched annotation, rollout and optimization budgets. Publish the per-round curve, including rejected candidates. A single successful update demonstrates one step. Multiple externally scored rounds and an unchanged anchor can test whether gains accumulate.
3. **Freeze the system that earned the result.** Record weights and adapters, planner, prompts, retrieval or skill-library snapshot, action decoder and normalizer, recovery code, controller, permitted sensors, runtime config and reset policy. Give the bundle a reproducible identity. Cold-load it and replay a fixed input within tolerance. Clear episode-local state between independent trials. Record whether a human or an automated rule selected the change, and which component is inherited.
4. **Run independent acceptance under its actual protocol.** Verify the current track, allowed observations and training sources, and permitted within-episode adaptation. RoboDojo uses public layouts for the main leaderboard and hidden layouts to check the consistency of submitted checkpoints. Unverified results are listed separately. Its repository README describes local debugging and score iteration. The paper and README describe the evaluation setup. The separate leaderboard protocol restricts the use of feedback from local or official rollouts on RoboDojo layouts to optimize submissions. Under this workflow, classify all public-layout scores as development data and use only official hidden-layout or internally sealed results as promotion evidence. Keep benchmark feedback subject to the applicable submission rules. [RoboDojo paper, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04434#S3.SS3) · [RoboDojo README](https://github.com/RoboDojo-Benchmark/RoboDojo/blob/main/README.md) · [Leaderboard protocol](https://robodojo-benchmark.com/leaderboard/protocol?lang=en)
   For real-robot acceptance, compare the candidate and incumbent in double-blind paired A/B trials. Match initial conditions within each pair, randomize run order and conceal version labels from both the operator and the outcome assessor. Have an independent custodian retain the assignment until scoring is locked. Record any loss of blinding. Source pending for the exact robotics protocol.

**Worked decision.** A synthetic panel has 12 complete pairs: 5 both succeed, 3 candidate-only, 1 parent-only and 3 both fail. The paired gain is 2/12, or 16.7 percentage points. A predeclared zero-loss requirement applies to designated retention cases and the single lost case is in that set. Retain the parent. If the panel came from official acceptance and its failure was used to change the recovery prompt, also mark that evidence as exposed and consult the protocol before any new submission.

**Decision record.** State the parent, candidate and anchor IDs, changed and frozen components, data exposure, threshold results, decision and rollback target. Label offline inference, handoff continuation, full simulation, controlled hardware, and organizer acceptance by the evidence actually obtained. Promotion within development can precede official acceptance.
